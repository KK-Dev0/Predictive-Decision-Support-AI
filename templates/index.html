      Fairness

      Do outcomes differ by age?

      The data has no sex or race column, but age is present and is a sensitive attribute in lending. Decisions on unseen borrowers were compared across three age groups, with age in the model and with age removed.

      • The burden of errors is uneven. {{ fair_note.young_fd | pct }} of good borrowers under 30 were declined versus {{ fair_note.old_fd | pct }} of those aged 60 and over{% if fair_note.ratio %}, about {{ fair_note.ratio | round(1) }} times as often{% endif %}.
      • Removing age does not fix it. Test ROC-AUC falls by only {{ "%.3f"|format(fair_note.auc_drop) }} and decline rates barely move. The likely reason is that other inputs carry age-related signal. That explanation was not tested directly.
      • Read with care. The youngest group is small, so its rates carry about a point of sampling noise. This is an audit, not a legal compliance review.

      About

      Method and limits

      Data: the Kaggle competition Give Me Some Credit, 150,000 anonymized US borrowers. Target: 90 or more days past due within two years. Pipeline: cleaning, five engineered features, imputing with missing-value flags, ANOVA feature selection, and a tuned {{ meta.model_name }} chosen by cross-validated ROC-AUC. Recommendation bands: decline at {{ t_star | pct }} or higher, manual review from {{ t_review | pct }}.

      Limitations

      • The data is from around 2011 and from one country, so results will not carry over directly elsewhere.
      • The decline threshold rests on an assumed 5:1 cost ratio, not on real lender data.
      • Probabilities are model estimates, not guarantees.
      • Real lending needs legal review, richer data and human oversight.
